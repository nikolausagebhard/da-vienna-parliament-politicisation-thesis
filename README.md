# Replication Archive: From Politicisation to Polarisation: Parliamentary Discourse in Austria, 1996–2022 

This repository contains the complete replication code and data processing pipeline for analyzing political animosity within high-salience immigration debates in the Austrian Nationalrat (Legislative Periods 20–27)[cite: 1, 3, 9]. The analytical framework integrates raw text aggregation, automated structural segment tokenization, large language model (LLM) classification via the Gemini 2.5 Flash API, and downstream econometric estimation[cite: 1, 4, 5, 8, 12].

## 📋 Pipeline Execution Order

To replicate the empirical findings of this thesis, execute the scripts sequentially according to their numeric prefixes (`01_` through `13_`). Each script is dependent on the data environments or file exports generated by its predecessor.

---

## 📂 Project Script Directory

### Phase 1: Corpus Extraction & Text Preprocessing

#### `01_Slice_Periods.R`
* **Purpose**: Ingests the raw `ParlaCAP-AT` speeches and sentence-level source data files (`.tsv`)[cite: 9]. It establishes the historical boundaries for legislative periods 20 to 27, programs the official structural seat distributions, and defines the programmatic party hex-color palette for all subsequent visualizations[cite: 9].
* **Core Outputs**: Filters and exports `immigration_high_salience.csv`, isolating parliamentary dates where immigration debates exceed a threshold of 10,000 words or constitute $\ge 10\%$ of the total daily text volume[cite: 9].

#### `02_DebateCreationFunctionPeriod_full.R`
* **Purpose**: Automated workflow loop that iterates over the identified high-salience debate dates[cite: 1]. It handles data-cleansing tasks (e.g., clearing out empty variables, dropping extraneous translation keys), mutates speech IDs, and merges scattered sentence records back into coherent text fields[cite: 1].
* **Core Outputs**: Generates full individual debate files (`Debate_[DATE].csv`) and initial cleaned topic-specific discussion exports[cite: 1].

#### `03_Tokenise All Speeches.R`
* **Purpose**: Implements text corpus noise reduction by targeting and stripping out standard formal parliamentary filler phrases (e.g., *"Meine Damen und Herren!"*, *"Hohes Haus!"*, *"Herr Präsident"*) using case-insensitive regular expressions[cite: 8]. It tokenizes the remaining raw text bodies into distinct sentences and bins them into uniform chunks of 5 continuous sentences per observation unit[cite: 8].
* **Core Outputs**: `Debates All Legislative Period 5 Sentences.csv`[cite: 8].

---

### Phase 2: Validation Sampling & LLM Benchmarking

#### `04_LLM Training Data Split.R`
* **Purpose**: Enforces reproducible sampling mechanics by fixing a pseudo-random seed (`set.seed(123)`)[cite: 6]. It extracts a validation core sample of 400 text segments out of the broader population to serve as the baseline for manual human annotation[cite: 6].
* **Core Outputs**: `training_standard_to_classify.csv` (and `.xls`) ready for manual coding[cite: 6].

#### `05_LLM Zero Shot Classifier.R`
* **Purpose**: Establishes the initial connection to the `gemini-2.5-flash` model via the Google AI Studio API (`httr2`)[cite: 4, 12]. Prompts the model to evaluate the 400-row sample for **Animosity** based on a strict system rule set (returning a binary `1` for hostility/derogation towards out-groups or a `0` for respectful/neutral disagreement) along with a short explanation[cite: 4].
* **Core Outputs**: `training_standrad_AI_Classified_0S.xls`[cite: 4].

#### `06_LLM Few Shot Classifier.R`
* **Purpose**: Enhances classification context by isolating high-confidence positive and negative examples from manual coding and structuring them into an in-context few-shot instruction block[cite: 2]. Uses the `caret` package to calculate confusion matrices, allowing for an absolute comparison of precision, recall, accuracy, and $F_1$-score deltas between the zero-shot and few-shot classification frameworks[cite: 2, 4].
* **Core Outputs**: `training_standrad_AI_Classified_FS.xls` and comparative validation tables[cite: 2].

---

### Phase 3: Large-Scale Corpus Annotation

#### `07_ZeroShotClassifierFull.R`
* **Purpose**: Scales the validated zero-shot pipeline to execute across the entire remaining unclassified corpus[cite: 12]. Implements anti-join logic to ignore records already manually evaluated, incorporates a mandatory API rate-limiting delay safety (`Sys.sleep(0.5)`), and builds try-catch blocks to catch network drops or processing errors[cite: 12].
* **Core Outputs**: Combines model results with manual evaluation frames to generate `Final Classified Speeches.xls`, the complete corpus of 11,129 scored segments[cite: 12].

---

### Phase 4: Structural Topic & Speaker Analytics

#### `08_Issue Salience across the Years .R`
* **Purpose**: Aggregates word counts and topic ratios across the longitudinal timeline[cite: 9]. Visualizes the absolute density and temporal shifts of immigration salience across various government regimes[cite: 9].
* **Core Outputs**: Time-series line plots displaying the frequency of high-salience legislative debates[cite: 9].

#### `09_Over_UnderRep_MigrationDiscussion.R`
* **Purpose**: Tests for structural representation disparities within immigration debates[cite: 9]. Compares a party's total floor speaking time and word volume against their actual legislative seat allocation inside the Nationalrat chamber[cite: 9].
* **Core Outputs**: Over- and under-representation index shares broken down by political faction[cite: 9].

#### `10_UniqueSpeakersbyParty.R`
* **Purpose**: Tallies the absolute diversity of political actors engaging on this topic[cite: 10, 11]. Tracks individual `speaker_id` footprints to determine the unique number of party speakers per legislative period, normalizing the result against the faction's seat density[cite: 11].
* **Core Outputs**: `SpeakerCounts.xls` and corresponding longitudinal "Speakers per Seat" line plots[cite: 11].

---

### Phase 5: Post-Processing & Econometric Modeling

#### `11_EndResult Manipulation.R`
* **Purpose**: Runs data enrichment and aggregation[cite: 3]. Resolves missing speaker metrics, drops minister records to isolate backbenchers/party representatives, collapses segment scores into speech-level averages (`LLM_score`), and relies on conditional logic (`case_when`) to historically map shifting coalition statuses (Government vs. Opposition) across periods[cite: 3].
* **Core Outputs**: `FinalResultsCollapsed.csv` and `.xls`, the definitive master data table for empirical testing[cite: 3].

#### `12_Measuring Animosity Deviation.R`
* **Purpose**: Calculates baseline party-period average animosity tracks over historical eras[cite: 7]. Generates behavioral variations (like maximum absolute speech deviations) and creates distribution visual profiles across parties[cite: 7].
* **Core Outputs**: Comparative party boxplots showing the distributional variance of animosity scores across legislative periods[cite: 7].

#### `13_Robustness Check.R`
* **Purpose**: Executes the primary causal and descriptive OLS linear models (`lm()`) grouped by legislative period[cite: 5]. Regresses speech-level animosity averages against institutional positioning (`party_status`) and ideological block definitions (`ideology`: Left vs. Right blocks)[cite: 3, 5].
* **Core Outputs**: Outputs structured side-by-side regression summary text tables via `stargazer` and generates faceted coefficient whisper plots mapping the OLS estimates with 95% confidence intervals[cite: 5].

## 🛠️ Environment & Requirements

* **Language Environment**: R (version 4.x or higher)
* **Required System Libraries**: To properly compile the Excel export outputs, a working installation of Perl is required by the `WriteXLS` engine.
* **R Package Dependencies**: `tidyverse` (`dplyr`, `stringr`, `tidyr`, `ggplot2`), `httr2`, `jsonlite`, `glue`, `caret`, `stargazer`, `readxl`, `WriteXLS`, `tidytext`, `purrr`, `car`, `broom`, `dotwhisker`[cite: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8].
* **API Credentials**: You must configure a private Google AI Studio API Key within your local workspace under the `GEMINI_API_KEY` assignment token prior to launching scripts `05`, `06`, and `07`[cite: 4, 12].
